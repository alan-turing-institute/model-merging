Alan Turing Institute — model merging

Summarisation, distillation & evaluation

Extending the disjoint-halves merging experiment to a generation task (XSum), and testing how much of merging’s cost distillation can repair — both arms evaluated through Inspect AI. The controls that followed withdrew the repair result and left a smaller, firmer one in its place.

Status — exploratory, and revised. The distillation recovery result reported on 14 September has been withdrawn: a no-teacher control, replicated over five seeds, reproduces it. Crime arms complete at n=1,077; XSum at n=1,000 and saturated.

1 — The question

Does merging give you parallel training for free?

Split a training set in two, fine-tune an expert on each half independently, merge the two experts into one set of weights. If the merge recovers the full-data model, fine-tuning parallelises across machines in a way gradient synchronisation does not — poor man's parallelism.

The pilot on Reddit crime classification said no: a 0.5/0.5 linear merge landed below the better of its own two inputs. This work asks two follow-on questions.

A

Does it hold on a generation task?

Classification can only fail one way — the wrong class. On summarisation a merge that drifts back toward base behaviour shows up stylistically: length and sentence count move before the score does.

B

Can distillation repair the damage?

If merging halves both task vectors, a cheap distillation pass — each expert teaching only its own half — might restore what was lost without any machine ever seeing all the data.

2 — Estimand

Recovery fraction, not raw accuracy

Per the project's preregistration, the quantity of interest is

R = (model − best_half) / (full − best_half)

Value of RInterpretation
R ≥ 1Reached the full-data model — parallelism works
0 < R < 1Merging bought something, but not the full model
R ≤ 0No better than keeping the better half and discarding the other

The denominator is full − best_half, not full − base. Retention against base flatters any half-data merge, because most of the distance from base to full is bought by any fine-tuning at all. best_half is the honest baseline: it is what a practitioner would otherwise ship.

One design decision worth noting. The prompt is rendered once, at data-prep time, into a prompt column carried in the dataset. The crime arm keeps its prompt wording in two files kept in sync by hand — and they have in fact drifted, differing by quote characters. Storing the rendered prompt removes that failure mode entirely.

4 — Evaluation

Both tasks moved onto Inspect AI

inspect-ai had been a declared dependency of this project since the start with nothing importing it. Both arms now run through it.

Built

Adapter support does not exist upstream. Inspect's HF provider contains no mention of peft, lora or adapter. Most of what this project evaluates is base+adapter, so without a custom provider every adapter would have to be materialised into a full ~8.1 GB model purely to be scored.

Gained

Standard error on ROUGE, and a browsable per-sample log. The hand-rolled evaluator reported no measure of uncertainty — which mattered here, since the project's earlier headline rested on a gap smaller than its own training noise.

Caught

Inspect's HF provider defaults do_sample to true. Left alone, every summarisation number would have been sampled while the classification numbers stayed greedy — silently noisier than the baseline it was being compared against. Pinned to greedy, and dtype pinned to bfloat16 to match the legacy path.

This is a high floor, and it reshaped an assumption. The arm was motivated partly by the expectation that a drifting merge would show up as preamble returning and one sentence becoming three. It cannot: the base model already emits exactly one sentence with no preamble. What fine-tuning has to buy is narrower than assumed — XSum's specific terseness, not basic instruction-following.

ROUGE measures conformity to a house style, not summary quality. The base model's summaries are frequently better summaries than the references; they simply are not XSum-shaped. Any retention figure should be read as "kept 90% of the house style it learned", not "90% as useful".

6 — Distillation

Each expert teaches its own half

The student is initialised from the merge — the condition that failed. The teachers are the two half-experts, each supplying logprobs only for the data it was trained on.

ComponentDetail
Teacher 1half-1 expert over half-1 training data
Teacher 2half-2 expert over half-2 training data
Student initthe linear merge of those two, + LoRA
Lossaxolotl KD, kd_alpha 0.9 / kd_ce_alpha 0.1 / T = 1.0

The shape matters as much as the result. Nothing here requires any machine to have seen all the data — the logprob pass shards exactly the way the training did. A distillation needing the whole dataset in one place would answer a different and far less interesting question.

And a control, which turned out to matter. A second arm distils the merge from itself over the same data, changing only where the targets come from. Without it, a positive result cannot distinguish "the experts restored what merging lost" from "a second training pass helps regardless of the teacher".

Axolotl's KD is offline: the trainer reads precomputed teacher logprobs from the dataset. The expensive step is therefore a forward pass of each teacher over its own half — cheap on classification (~5 minutes per half, single-token targets), roughly 30× more on summarisation.

7 — Headline result

Merging costs. A cheap repair fixes it — and the teacher makes it reliable.

Reddit crime classification · accuracy · n = 1,077 · full held-out test set

The merge is worse than either of its own inputs (R = −0.46), reproducing the pilot’s negative result at full power. A one-epoch supervised pass over the union of the two halves repairs it to roughly full-data parity. The whisker on that bar is the seed range across five runs — its upper end passes the full-data model.

!

The teacher’s effect is variance, not magnitude

Five seeds per arm at measured loss weights: distillation reaches R = +1.03 ± 0.03, the no-teacher control +0.76 ± 0.20. The means differ by 0.011 (p ≈ 0.04); the variance differs 50-fold (p ≈ 0.001). Every distilled seed lands within 0.003 of full-data training; the control’s worst falls 0.019 below. The defensible claim is that distillation makes the repair reliable.

§

And it is a warm start, not parallelism

The repair pass trains on the union of both halves. A machine that can run it already holds all the data, so the property the project set out to preserve — that no machine sees everything — does not survive. The compute saving is real; the framing has to change.

See slide 13. The teacher signal behind these numbers was misaligned by one position; the reliability claim is suspended pending a re-run.

An earlier version of this deck reported R = +0.78 for expert-taught distillation and +0.39 for self-distillation, and attributed the difference to the experts’ knowledge. The control’s mean is +0.76 with no teacher at all, and seed 1 lands on 0.9610 — the exact accuracy that claim was built on. Both numbers were single draws from a distribution 0.56 wide; two standard deviations of seed noise is 0.40, and the decomposition rested on a gap of 0.39.

The preregistration specifies five seeds for precisely this reason, and the exploratory arm was run at n = 1 regardless. The finding did not survive the replication the design already required. Recorded in PREREGISTRATION.md under Deviations, 2026-09-18.

9 — XSum

The generation arm is healthy — and saturated

Lengths confirm the arm is sound — 19–19.4 words against a 21-word reference, one sentence each. Fine-tuning does what it should: +0.124 ROUGE-1 over base, p = 0.0002.

Finding

Half the data buys 95% of what all the data buys (+0.1176 of +0.1235). The entire gap between the better half and the full-data model is 0.0059 ROUGE-1, and it is not significant (p = 0.058). On crime that gap was 0.0381 — six times larger.

That gap is the denominator of R. The merge lands at R = −0.34, directionally consistent with crime’s −0.46, but dividing by a quantity indistinguishable from zero makes it unstable — and merge-vs-best-half is itself not significant (p = 0.44). XSum at 8,000 examples is a weak instrument for this question: there is almost nothing for merging to lose or recover. That is a boundary condition on the crime finding, not a contradiction of it — and it says the effect depends on being in a data regime where quantity still matters.

The opposite of the same-task result. Merging two disjoint halves of one task loses to the better half (R = −0.46). Merging two different experts retains 99.6% of one and 98.6% of the other — and does so despite the XSum expert alone scoring 0.4596 on crime, 29 points below the untuned base. Weight averaging repairs what one adapter did to the other’s task.

÷

The 96% tier averages; the 99% tier adds

Everything near 96% — linear, SLERP, task-arithmetic at 0.5 — halves each task vector’s magnitude and yields a diluted version of both. The 99% tier applies both deltas at full strength or trims conflicts and sign-resolves. The vectors are near-orthogonal, so adding them whole works where averaging does not.

And a trap the averages hide. arcee-fusion looks near-best on crime at 99.2%, but holds only 88.4% of the summariser at 25.2 words against the expert’s 19.4 — it bought classification back by drifting toward the base model’s verbose output. Only the length column shows it.

11 — Crowding

What crowds is the direction, not the count

Task count held at two; adapter count varied · the third adapter is a second crime-pointing expert
AdaptersMethodCrimeXSum R1Words
2task-arithmetic w=1.00.97310.399318.8
3task-arithmetic w=1.00.53480.394118.8
2TIES0.97030.399419.1
3TIES0.95170.403819.7

A third adapter collapses task-arithmetic at weight 1.0 — crime falls to below the untuned base model (0.7502), and below every component it was built from: the crime half-experts score 0.9294 and 0.9313 alone, and even their own failing merge manages 0.9136. TIES, which splits weight across adapters, loses 0.019.

→

The damage is directional

Summarisation is untouched in both merges — 0.3941 and 0.4038, generation length unchanged. The three-adapter set applies two crime-pointing adapters at full weight and one XSum adapter, so the crime direction gets double displacement and collapses while XSum, at its normal single dose, is fine.

So the question to ask before merging N adapters is not what N is, but how many of them point the same way. task-arithmetic won the two-way sweep precisely because adding whole vectors preserves both skills — and that same property makes it fail the moment any direction repeats. TIES was 0.3 points behind at n = 2 and is the one that survives a third model.

Not tested: the task-diversity axis. Everything here holds task count at two, so repeated directions may be the whole explanation for the eight-task study’s ~24 points of damage — or not. Distinguishing them needs new experts on new tasks.

The router itself discriminates: probing top-1 expert choice over medical and general prompts gives a separation of +0.215, so tokens are not being assigned at random.

→

Routing cannot produce these numbers

A router’s worst case is sending every token to the weaker expert — which would floor HellaSwag at Meditron’s 72.93. The MoE scores 45.63, twenty-seven points below that floor, on a commonsense task where neither expert is weak and medical routing is irrelevant. A score beneath both parents on all six is a broken model, not a routing trade-off.

→

The same two experts merge densely without difficulty

LERP beats both parents on all six benchmarks with the identical pair. So the deficit belongs to the MoE construction, not to the choice of experts — which is what makes it worth diagnosing rather than abandoning.

One evaluation outstanding. These scores were produced with ignore_mismatched_sizes, the flag that already destroyed a 131-million-value embedding tensor on the LERP merge and drove MMLU to chance. Llama-2 (32,000 tokens) and Meditron (32,017) mismatch by construction, so a strict re-run without the flag is queued: a hard error means the table above is an artefact, and a clean reproduction means the merge really is this bad.

Not composable with the rest of this deck. mergekit-moe emits Mixtral, DeepSeek and Qwen architectures only — Gemma-3 is unsupported — so this arm had to change model family and cannot be compared directly against the crime and XSum results.

13 — Correction

The teacher was misaligned in every distillation run behind these slides

Axolotl infers where teacher distributions belong from their count alone. Ours were one row short, so every distribution was applied to the token after the one it described. With teacher and student the same model — where a correct alignment must give zero — mean KL was 22.97 nats; corrected, 0.0000.

→

The project’s own controls could not have caught it

One control shifted the content of a fixed-length array, which cannot move the offset its length implies. Another saw the symptom directly — a distillation loss stuck at 8–20 with identical teacher and student — and concluded the reported loss simply was not a per-token KL. It was, and it was reporting this. A teacher-equals-student check costs seconds, cannot be satisfied by a self-consistent producer, and is now a required gate before any distillation run.

Re-runs are under way on a different machine. The Azure subscription is read-only, so the arms moved to Isambard-AI and the experts had to be retrained — the registry artefacts cannot be fetched. Absolute figures on these slides will not carry across; the re-run is internally consistent and is what the recovery fraction needs.

14 — Verification

Four checks caught defects. The one we lacked cost a week.

Scale check

Derives the step count a dataset implies and compares it to the adapter's checkpoints. It established that artifacts selected by name, then by size, then by registration timestamp were in fact 200-example smoke runs registered under production names — and separately confirmed a colleague's teachers as genuine at 808 steps against ~810 expected.

Alignment check

Teacher logprobs must line up with the tokens they describe. An off-by-one still trains, still shows a falling loss, and teaches noise. Counting tokens cannot detect it — a shifted list has the right length. The check compares top-1 agreement aligned vs shifted: 0.446 vs 0.018 on XSum, and it rejects a deliberately corrupted dataset at 0.000 vs 0.944.

Length check

The first XSum run measured a broken trainer, and only the length columns showed it. Gemma ends turns with <end_of_turn>, not <eos>; axolotl warned that the terminator would not be trained and continued. Those models ran to the 64-token cap — 51 words against a 21-word reference — and the full-data model scored worse than no fine-tuning (0.262 vs 0.281). Reported on ROUGE alone, “merging beats full-data training on summarisation” would have looked like a finding.

Handover check

A colleague's Llama self-distilled model does not generate. It degenerates under chat template, raw prompt, greedy, and sampling-with-repetition-penalty alike — while gemma summarises coherently through the identical code path minutes apart. Its ROUGE of 0.000 means "produced no words", not "summarised badly", and was not reported as a score.

One correction worth recording: the alignment check's first threshold was a ratio calibrated on XSum's ~30-token targets, and it rejected a correctly aligned dataset on the single-token classification task. A safety net that cries wolf gets switched off, so the rule was replaced with two criteria that hold at any target length.

Degeneracy check

The one we lacked, and it cost a week. Three distilled summarisers scored 0.332–0.338 against a merge at 0.3970, and it read as “distillation degrades the model”. It was not. Roughly one output in ten had collapsed — empty, a bare full stop, or one token repeated to the 64-token cap (Bes Bes Bes…) — while the surviving summaries stayed competitive. An empty output scores zero, so a tenth of the test set failing that way produced most of the gap. Counting empties and repeated words takes one pass over the predictions and would have shown this on day one.

The cause was a copied hyperparameter. kd_alpha 0.9 / kd_ce_alpha 0.1 came from a published run on a different task. Those weights encode an assumption about the ratio of two loss magnitudes; here they are ~8 and ~1.4, so the cross-entropy term that teaches the model to emit <end_of_turn> carried about a fiftieth of the gradient. Setting the weights from the measured magnitudes (0.2 / 1.0) took all four degeneracy counts to zero and recovered the entire 0.061 ROUGE-1 deficit. Lowering the learning rate could not: that scales both terms and leaves the ratio alone.

A sixth check is the no-teacher control on slide 7. It caught nothing broken — it withdrew a result, which is the more expensive kind of catch.

15 — For stakeholders

Practical implications

1

Do not ship a naive merge — but know where it matters

On classification, a 0.5/0.5 linear merge is worse than keeping the better half and discarding the other. On XSum, where half the data already buys 95% of the benefit, merging costs almost nothing because there is almost nothing to lose. The harm scales with how much the second half of your data is actually worth.

2

A cheap repair reaches full-data parity — as a warm start, not sharding

One epoch over the combined data brings the merge back to full-data parity. Across five seeds: a plain supervised pass recovers R = +0.76 ± 0.20, and adding a correctly weighted teacher gives +1.03 ± 0.03. The teacher’s contribution is reliability, not accuracy — a 50-fold drop in seed variance, with every distilled run landing within 0.003 of the full-data model. But the repair trains on the combined data, so this is a compute saving from a warm start, not the property that no machine sees everything.

3

Cost is modest and predictable

The teacher pass is the only new expense: ~5 minutes per half for single-token targets, scaling roughly with target length. Teacher logprobs shard cleanly — each expert scores only its own data. The repair pass does not: it trains on the union, and that is the step that breaks the sharding property.

4

Merging different experts is close to free — watch repeated directions

A classifier merged with a summariser keeps 99.6% and 98.6% of each. The harm in the halves arms comes from merging two models trained on the same thing. So before merging N adapters, ask how many point the same way: with task-arithmetic at full weight one repeated direction collapsed that task to below the untuned base, while leaving the other untouched. TIES splits weight and degrades gracefully.

5

Evaluation is infrastructure, not an afterthought

Four defects — smoke artifacts under production names, a trainer that never learned to stop, a handed-over model that cannot generate, and a control that reframed the headline — were caught by checks rather than by inspection. Each would have produced a clean-looking, wrong number.

16 — Limits

What this does not establish

!

Exploratory, not confirmatory. The project's preregistration licenses three specific runs, and the distillation arm is not one of them. It is recorded as a dated deviation and generates a hypothesis rather than testing one. Nothing here bears on the preregistered H1–H4.

!

Seeds exist for two conditions only. The no-teacher control and the balanced distillation arm have five seeds each on crime. Every other number — the on-policy arm, both XSum arms, every cross-task merge — is a single run against a measured noise floor of ~0.008 accuracy. Differences below roughly 0.015 in those rows are not resolvable.

!

Loss weights were set from measured magnitudes, not swept. 0.9/0.1, copied from a published run on another task, cost 0.061 ROUGE-1 and produced degeneracy in one output in ten; 0.2/1.0, derived from cross-entropy near 1.4 against a top-k KL near 8, removed both. Two points on a trade-off, not a sweep — and the values do not transfer to a new task.

!

Task diversity is untested. The cross-task work holds task count at two throughout. Repeated directions may fully explain the eight-task study’s ~24 points of damage, or an independent effect from genuinely distinct tasks may exist. Nothing measured here separates them.

!

Two headline claims were withdrawn mid-study. R = +0.78 for expert-taught distillation, and then its replacement, “distillation contributes nothing”. The first was one draw from a distribution 0.56 wide; the second was measured at loss weights that proved to be an artefact. Both are recorded as dated deviations in the preregistration.

!

Baselines are evaluated once and reused across arms. The merge, both halves and the full-data model are fixed artifacts scored on a fixed test set with greedy decoding, so every arm and every seed shares one evaluation of each rather than recomputing identical numbers. Only the student is evaluated per arm and per seed. That is deliberate — it saves roughly 80 minutes of GPU per seed — but it means the baseline rows carry no independent variance estimate, and any evaluation-side error in them would propagate to every comparison at once.

Since resolved. All three XSum distillation arms have run. As predicted they are uninformative about merging — the task is saturated — but they diagnosed the loss-weighting fault above. The on-policy arm proved near-vacuous by construction: with the teacher equal to the student’s own initialisation the divergence starts at zero, and it changed 5 of 1,077 examples.

17 — Recommendations

Future work, in priority order

1

Measure the noise floor, then re-test. Run the preregistered seed sweep (42–46). Until the within-condition spread is known, no gap of a few points — including this one — is safely interpretable.

2

Seed everything before comparing anything. Seed noise here is ±0.20 in R — wider than most contrasts this project has drawn. Five seeds per condition is the preregistered minimum and should gate any future claim, including the distillation arm, which is still n = 1.

3

Sweep kd_alpha. One point on the teacher/label trade-off is not a curve. The weight is the single most consequential untuned knob in the arm.

4

Re-run XSum in a regime where data quantity matters. At 4,000 examples per half the task is saturated and cannot test merging at all. Smaller halves would restore the full-vs-half gap and make the arm informative — and the preregistration’s power analysis should account for this, since it is a property of the task and the data budget, not of merging.

5

Resolve the crime prompt drift. Training and evaluation differ by quote characters. It affects all models equally so comparisons hold, but absolute numbers are not portable, and the fix belongs with a re-run.

6

Harden the training loop. Axolotl hangs after saving, reliably, on this setup — three trainings, three hangs, each needing manual intervention. Writing the completion marker when the adapter appears rather than when the process exits would make these a non-event.

7

Unblock the Llama strand. Request gated access (no base means no floor to score against), and ask for the missing half-experts, merge, and training config — the handed-over model alone cannot answer whether distillation beat the merge it came from.

18 — In one line

Merging costs. A one-epoch repair fixes it. The teacher makes it dependable.

A linear merge of two disjoint-half experts scored below both its inputs (R = −0.46) — the project’s most robust result. One supervised epoch over the union of the halves repairs it to roughly full-data parity, but unreliably: R = +0.76 ± 0.20 across five seeds. Adding a correctly weighted teacher gives R = +1.03 ± 0.03 — the same repair, with a fiftieth of the variance.

And a second result, from merging different experts rather than halves. One crime classifier merged with one XSum summariser retains 99.6% and 98.6% of each — merging different skills is close to free, where merging two halves of one task is not. What crowds is the direction, not the count: a third adapter pointing the same way as an existing one collapses that task to below the untuned base while leaving the other untouched.

Two earlier versions of this deck got this wrong in opposite directions — first crediting the experts on single-seed runs, then denying them on runs whose loss weights had been copied from another task. Both were measurement failures rather than results. Two caveats stand regardless: the repair needs the combined data on one machine, so it is a warm-start saving rather than the parallelism property the project set out to show; and on XSum none of it is testable, because half the data already buys 95% of the benefit.
