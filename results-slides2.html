Model Merging Investigation · Phase 2 · 1 — Goals

Azure Reproduction & Scale-Up

Multi-node training is expensive — is merging a cheaper surrogate? Data-parallel merging (training small/medium models independently, then combining weights) could be a "poor man's" alternative to synchronized multi-node distributed training. This phase validates the toolchain first: reproduce a known local result on Azure ML, then confirm it scales.

  • Goal 1 — reproduce: take the local Qwen2.5-0.5B merge experiment (4 methods × 3 baselines, scored on ARC-Easy/PIQA) and get matching results on Azure ML infrastructure.
  • Goal 2 — scale: once validated, rerun the identical pipeline on a 3x larger model (Qwen2.5-1.5B) with zero config changes beyond swapping checkpoint names.

Fixed

A

DARE-TIES isn't just riskier — it's non-reproducible

Its ARC-Easy accuracy swung 0.5084→0.5762 (6.8pp) between identical local/Azure runs of the same config. Cause: MergeKit's DARE dropout mask (torch.bernoulli, sparsify.py:131) is unseeded by default — pass --random-seed, or treat any single DARE-TIES result as one noisy draw, not ground truth.

3 — Reproduction: bugs found while reproducing the paper

Llama-2-7B-Chat and Meditron-7B don't even share a vocab size

Llama-2-7B-Chat has a 32,000-token vocabulary; Meditron-7B (continued-pretrained from Llama-2 for medical text) has 32,017 — 17 extra special tokens. Both LERP and DARE-TIES broke on exactly the tensors this mismatch touches (embed_tokens, lm_head); TIES and SLERP happened to take a code path that handles it safely.

Fixed

LERP: config lied about the checkpoint's own shape. Direct tensor inspection showed LERP's merged embed_tokens/lm_head data was statistically healthy (mean/std matched TIES's own values closely) — but its config.json declared vocab_size: 32017 (copied from Meditron) while the actual saved tensors were [32000, 4096] (Llama's shape). Loading with ignore_mismatched_sizes=True to work around the resulting crash didn't just patch 17 rows — it discarded and randomly reinitialized the entire, otherwise-correct tensor. Patching vocab_size to 32000 recovered scores from near-random (MMLU 22.99%) to within 0.1pt of the paper (MMLU 48.37% vs. 48.44%).

Fixed

DARE-TIES: an fp16 overflow, not just bad luck. Tensor inspection found embed_tokens.weight and lm_head.weight were 100% NaN — 131 million values each, corrupted during the merge itself. Root cause: dare_ties defaults to rescale=True, dividing by the L1 norm of whatever survives its random Bernoulli mask; for these two vocab-mismatched tensors specifically, an unlucky mask draw left a near-zero surviving norm, producing a rescale factor that overflowed float16's ~65,504 max to Inf, then NaN. Switching to bfloat16 (~3e38 max) recovered 5 of 6 benchmarks to within ~2pts of the paper — preserving DARE's actual rescale behavior rather than disabling it.

B

MergeKit's own paper replicates — but only after two more real bugs

Same story as the OLMo-2/Gemma-3 investigation: MergeKit's core averaging math is sound, but edge cases (mismatched vocabularies, in this case) surface real implementation gaps that silently produce broken checkpoints rather than erroring loudly. Every family/paper we've stress-tested this pipeline against has turned up at least one genuine, fixable bug.

3 — Reproduction: verdict

So — can we trust Azure results?

  • Local vs. Azure, same config (Qwen2.5-0.5B): Linear, TIES, and Arcee Fusion agree closely across both environments. DARE-TIES diverges by 6.8pp on ARC-Easy — traced to its own unseeded RNG, not an Azure-vs-local discrepancy.
  • Azure vs. the published paper (Llama-2-7B-Chat + Meditron-7B): all four methods land within 1.3–2.9 points of the paper's own Table 1, once two real bugs (a config metadata bug in LERP, an fp16 overflow in DARE-TIES) were found and fixed.
✓

Yes — with two standing caveats

Always pass an explicit seed for DARE-TIES (its dropout mask is non-deterministic by default), and always sanity-check a merged checkpoint's tensor health and config metadata directly — a model can load and "succeed" while silently carrying corrupted or mislabeled weights.

C

0.5B had a real cost; 1.5B has none — if anything, a gain

Every method's accuracy is higher at 1.5B than at 0.5B, merged or not — but merged models specifically go from below-base (0.5B) to significantly above-base on ARC-Easy (1.5B). Pooled GEE: ARC-Easy OR=1.002 (p=0.894), PIQA OR=1.003 (p=0.828) — no detectable pooled effect either direction at 1.5B, versus 0.5B's real cost (OR=0.937, p=1.2e-05 on ARC-Easy). Model capacity appears to absorb merge interference — encouraging for merging as a "poor man's parallelism" surrogate, if the effect holds at larger scale still.

Timing scaled as expected: 60m28s total eval time for all 7 models (~8m30s each) vs. 22m09s at 0.5B — a ~2.8x increase for a 3x parameter increase, consistent with compute scaling roughly linearly with model size on the same T4.

D

Best current explanation: two independent MergeKit bugs, not a unifying rule

No shared architectural property distinguishes the two broken families from the four working ones. Most likely: MergeKit's OLMo-2 and Gemma-3 architecture support each have their own undiscovered gap — the same category of bug as the already-fixed q_norm/k_norm issue — rather than a deep truth about which architectures can be merged at all.

Bugs & issues found (1/2)

Every stage of this pipeline surfaced a real bug

Fixed

MergeKit PyPI 0.1.4 crashes against transformers 5.x — a Pydantic forward-reference error. The release predates when MergeKit's own dependency constraints were updated for transformers 5.x. Fixed by installing from GitHub main.

Fixed

CPU-only compute is ~20x too slow for eval-heavy runs — first attempt projected ~7 hours for a job that took 43 minutes on a single T4.

Fixed

DARE-TIES is non-reproducible by default — its Bernoulli dropout mask is unseeded unless --random-seed is passed, so re-running the identical config gives a genuinely different merged model each time (confirmed: local vs. Azure DARE-TIES differed by 6.8pp on ARC-Easy).

Fixed

Gemma's large vocab overflows T4 VRAM during TIES/DARE-TIES merging — the sparsify step's full-tensor argsort needs more memory than a 16GB card has for Gemma's 256k-vocab embedding/lm_head tensors. Fixed by merging on CPU.

Bugs & issues found (2/2)

...and the rest

Fixed (took 3 iterations)

Torch/CUDA version tightrope: pinned cu121 resolved torch 2.5.1 (too old for a safetensors safety check, broke OLMo's DPO variant); dropping the pin resolved a build needing newer CUDA than the T4 driver supports. Fixed by pinning cu124 (torch 2.6.0) — satisfies both constraints.

Upstream, workaround applied

MergeKit's OLMo-2 architecture definition was missing q_norm/k_norm weights (#602) — a confirmed, real bug, and a contributing factor. But applying the open fix (PR #699) did not resolve the collapse — merged OLMo models are still near-random. There is at least one more, still-unidentified issue beyond this one.

Fixed

Silent partial failure via a pipefail gotcha — lm_eval | tee log reports tee's exit code, not lm_eval's, so set -e alone didn't stop the script when an eval crashed; the job reported "success" with a missing result and a swallowed downstream traceback. Fixed with set -euo pipefail.

Found, not yet fixed

GSM8K's generation-based eval is disproportionately expensive — ~22-27s/item on T4 (vs. ~1-2s/item for loglikelihood tasks), timing out a 90-minute job at ~6% complete. Needs either a much longer timeout, a faster backend (e.g. vLLM), or dropping GSM8K from routine sweeps.

Found, not yet persisted

Merged model weights weren't being saved — only eval results were uploaded as job outputs, so every benchmark expansion or bug investigation required a full re-merge. Fixed going forward with a merged_models job output.

Fixed

LERP config metadata lied about the merged checkpoint's own tensor shape (found during the MergeKit-paper reproduction, Section 3) — config.json declared Meditron's vocab_size while the saved tensors were Llama's shape. Patched the metadata directly rather than discarding the tensor.

Fixed

DARE-TIES's default rescale overflows fp16 to NaN on mismatched-vocab tensors (same reproduction) — an unlucky Bernoulli mask draw left a near-zero surviving norm, and the resulting rescale factor overflowed float16. Fixed via dtype: bfloat16.

6 — Conclusions

Five findings, together

A

DARE-TIES isn't just riskier — it's non-reproducible

Its unseeded random dropout mask means any single DARE-TIES result is one noisy draw, not ground truth — pass --random-seed, or run it multiple times.

B

MergeKit's own paper replicates — but only after two more real bugs

Reproducing the toolkit's founding case study (Llama-2-7B-Chat + Meditron-7B) surfaced a config-metadata bug in LERP and an fp16-overflow-to-NaN bug in DARE-TIES, both triggered by the two source models' mismatched vocab sizes. Once fixed, all four methods land within a few points of the paper's published numbers.

C

0.5B had a real merge cost; 1.5B had none — if anything, a gain

Model capacity appears to absorb merge interference — encouraging for merging as a "poor man's parallelism" surrogate, if the effect holds at larger scale still.

D

OLMo-2 and Gemma-3 both collapse — but not for a unifying architectural reason

Two hypotheses (QK-norm, post-norm) were tested directly and both disproven — Qwen3 and OLMo-3 share those traits and merge cleanly (OLMo-3's merge even beat its own source checkpoints). Best explanation: two separate, undiscovered MergeKit implementation gaps, not a deep rule about which architectures can be merged.

E

Three families, three different qualitative outcomes

Llama-1B doesn't look like Qwen-0.5B despite similar size — architecture/training-recipe differences matter at least as much as parameter count, arguing against a single blanket "merging is safe above N params" rule.

7 — What next

What next

01

Consult with stakeholders on which of the four directions below to prioritize.

02

Keep experimenting with new methods, modifications, and new data.

03

Poor Man's Training Parallelism — the original thesis: continued-pretraining on parallel data shards (Dolma/RedPajama/SlimPajama), then merging, as a surrogate for multi-node distributed training.

04

Merging at Scale — repeat this family comparison at sizes that matter for deployment, not just sub-3B proxies.

05

Expert Merging (sparse MoE) — merge as a way to construct or combine mixture-of-experts models rather than dense ones.

06

Identifying knowledge preserved via merging — go beyond aggregate benchmark accuracy to understand which capabilities specifically survive, transfer, or get destroyed by each merge method.
